Digital platforms often subcontract self-employed workers. We develop an equilibrium labor-market model to quantify Spain's 2021 Riders’ Law, which sanctions platforms in the online food delivery sector for hiring self-employed couriers, known as riders. Riders in the model trade off flexibility and employability as subcontracted against higher wages and guaranteed hours of work as employees. We find that the reform increases the employee share by 2 percentage points but that this rise falls short of absorbing job losses among the self-employed. Overall, riders and consumers experience welfare losses due to lower average hourly wages and employment in the sector as a whole as a result of the sanctions. However, we find that redistributing sanction revenue as lump-sum transfers to riders offset those losses.
